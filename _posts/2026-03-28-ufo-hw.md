## Visualization 1: Frequency of Reported UFO Shapes
This bar chart visualizes the distribution of different UFO shapes reported in the dataset. I used a horizontal bar chart where the y-axis represents the **nominal** encoding for the UFO shapes, and the x-axis represents the **quantitative** count of sightings. The shapes are sorted in descending order to quickly identify the most common types, such as "light" and "circle."

For design choices, I utilized a consistent **'steelblue'** color for all bars to maintain visual clarity and focus the viewer's attention on the comparative frequency rather than individual categories. Regarding data transformations, the count aggregation was handled directly within the Altair encoding. I also ensured the data was read from the raw URL to comply with the project requirements.

## Visualization 2: Sighting Duration Trends (Interactive)

The second visualization explores the relationship between the date of a sighting and its duration. I used **temporal** encoding for the x-axis (Date) and **quantitative** encoding for the y-axis (Duration in seconds). Because the duration values vary significantly, I applied a **logarithmic scale** to the y-axis to make the distribution more readable. The points are colored based on the **nominal** 'shape' variable, using the default Altair color scheme to differentiate between UFO types.

**Interactivity Features:**
For this plot, I implemented two interactive elements: a **tooltip** and a **year selection slider**. The tooltip allows users to hover over any data point to see specific details like the city, shape, and exact duration. The year slider helps filter the dense dataset, allowing users to focus on sightings from a specific year. This interactivity makes the visualization much more effective at managing overplotting and enables a more granular exploration of the data.
